Accountability where technology begins

Abusive technologies often start as research and are then developed and sold by companies. When research violates ethical standards, journals, publishers, and universities have the power — and the duty — to correct the record, up to retraction. When companies supply technology used for abuse, they must answer for where it ends up. We document both, from published sources, and press for correction.

Approach

How we work

01

Document

We examine published research and commercial practices against established ethical standards: consent, harm, and the rights of the people involved. Every claim is sourced.

02

Engage

We bring cases to the editors, publishers, institutions, and companies responsible, and give them the chance to respond and act.

What this work has achieved so far

  • About 50 retractions of articles based on DNA or facial data from vulnerable populations collected without valid consent.
  • New ethics requirements for human-subject research across IEEE’s journals, and new Nature Portfolio guidelines on research involving vulnerable groups.

Reports

Each report traces a surveillance technology to the institutions that built it and the companies that supply it, from published documents. Every claim is graded by confidence, from verified to unverified, and a copy of every cited source, or its record, is archived on Zenodo. Each report is published as a web page, a PDF, and a Markdown file.
